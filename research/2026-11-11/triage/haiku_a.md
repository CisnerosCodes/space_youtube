## 1. Chandra (chandra.txt)

- **Insight:** A hidden companion keeps pulling material off a giant star every 113 days, which drives its repeated outbursts.
- **Quote:** "a previously hidden companion object that keeps pulling material away from the massive star every 113 days as they orbit one another."
- **Image:** https://assets.science.nasa.gov/dynamicimage/assets/science/missions/chandra/2026/at2016blu.jpg?w=2048&h=2048&fit=clip&crop=faces%2Cfocalpoint
  Credit: "X-ray: NASA/CXC/Univ. of Virgina/M. Aghakhanloo et al.; Optical: SDSS; Image Processing: NASA/CXC/SAO/P. Edmonds"
- **Next:** none (no dated upcoming event on the page).
- **Risks:** The companion is unidentified ("either a stellar-mass black hole, or ... a neutron star"). "First X-ray detection" rests on one study, and the star is a single dot in the composite. "Best explanation" is a hedge. Numbers are "at least" (33 solar masses, 27 bursts). Keep "every 113 days" separate from "about three times a year." Copy the credit's "Virgina" spelling as given.

## 2. ESCAPADE (escapade.txt)

- **Insight:** NASA used a powered Earth flyby, a gravity slingshot plus an engine burn, to send ESCAPADE toward Mars.
- **Quote:** "This is the first time NASA has used a powered Earth flyby to propel a spacecraft toward Mars"
- **Image:** https://assets.science.nasa.gov/dynamicimage/assets/science/missions/escapade/ESCAPADE-B_TMI_07.jpg?w=2048&h=1365&fit=clip&crop=faces%2Cfocalpoint
  Credit: "Alan Toth/UC Berkeley Space Sciences Laboratory". The page pairs this credit with a different photo, so the match to this URL is unconfirmed. The trajectory visual is an animation, credited "Advanced Space/Matt Bolliger", not a photo.
- **Next:** "culminating with their Mars orbit insertion burns planned for Sept. 7 and Sept. 9, 2027."
- **Risks:** The image is weak: staff at monitors, not the spacecraft. "First time" is NASA's own claim, quoted from Asal Naseri. The 2027 date is a plan, and the mission is still in cruise. Altitudes ("about 1,000 miles" and "650 miles") are easy to overstate.
