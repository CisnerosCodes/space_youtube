**1. lisa.txt (LISA gravitational-wave exoplanets)**
- Insight: A planet orbiting a double white dwarf changes the gravitational-wave signal, and LISA could read it.
- Quote: "For LISA, this modulation will appear as sidebands, secondary signals at both higher and lower frequencies than the primary one."
- Image: https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/lisa/news/2026/l3harris-award-for-test-telescope/LISA_key_visual_ESA_lg_ac.jpg/jcr:content/renditions/cq5dam.web.1280.1280.jpeg | credit "ESA"
- Next: "Led by ESA (European Space Agency), the LISA mission is slated for launch in the mid-2030s."
- Risks: The image is an artist's concept, so it does not count as a real photo. "Mid-2030s" is a vague date. "10 million" and "10,000" are estimates ("Astronomers estimate"). The planet detection is a predicted method, not an observed one.

**2. rubies.txt (Rubies on Mars?)**
- Insight: The corundum crystals that give rubies their color are helping Perseverance learn about Mars' past.
- Quote: "While we haven’t found collectable rubies on Mars yet, the little corundum crystals are helping us learn more about Mars’ past."
- Image: https://assets.science.nasa.gov/dynamicimage/assets/science/missions/mars2020/2026/perseverance-rover-updates/Mars_Perseverance_SIF_2023_0846549705_738EBY_N0930000SRLC02503_0000LMJ.jpg?w=1648&h=1200&fit=clip&crop=faces%2Cfocalpoint | credit "NASA/JPL-Caltech". The photo shows a float rock, not a ruby or corundum.
- Next: none on the page.
- Risks: The hook "Rubies on Mars?" overstates it, because the page says no collectable rubies have been found. The story is a hedged, single-team result. The wavelengths and the "Two years ago" timing need to be stated exactly.

**3. oldest_rock.txt (Newest Sample, Oldest Rock?)**
- Insight: Perseverance's newest sample, "Singita," may have formed deep in the crust and been hit by impacts.
- Quote: "Textures in the rock suggest that it likely formed deep in the crust and was modified and excavated by at least one, and possibly multiple, large impact events."
- Image: https://assets.science.nasa.gov/dynamicimage/assets/science/missions/mars2020/2026/perseverance-rover-updates/Mars_Perseverance_ZL0_2018_0846089083_331EBY_N0920000ZCAM05068_1100LMJ.jpg?w=1648&h=1200&fit=clip&crop=faces%2Cfocalpoint | credit "NASA/JPL-Caltech/ASU". This is a real photo of the sample.
- Next: none on the page.
- Risks: The title is a question, and the page says "may be among the oldest rocks," so the claim is hedged. The "66th successful abrasion" and status-report content leans toward a routine update. The story depends on rock texture interpretation, not a measured age.
