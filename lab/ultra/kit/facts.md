## 5. Hard limits (from the research risks)

- No sampling year on screen. NASA says 'no earlier than December 2028', ESA says 2030, JAXA gives only fiscal years. Arrival (August 2027) and return (2031) are safe.
- Do not say or show that MMX 'phoned home', is 'healthy' or deployed its solar arrays: no primary source confirms it yet. 'Launched', 'separated', 'injected into the predetermined orbit' are confirmed.
- Launch date: Oct 20 in Japan (JST) is Oct 19 in UTC, Europe and the US. If a date chip appears, give both or use JST with the time.
- Keep the hook's either/or: crash into Mars OR break up into a ring, in about 50 million years. Never show Phobos hitting Mars soon.
- 'First piece of Phobos ever brought home' is supported (F06). Do not upgrade it to 'first sample of Mars': JAXA only says MMX 'may' return Mars material inside the Phobos soil (F33), and Mars meteorites already exist on Earth.
- Gravity is stated three ways (JAXA 1/1700, CNES 1,800 times weaker, DLR less than a thousandth). Use the narration's '15 grams' (ESA). If a ratio is shown, use JAXA's 1/1700 and say it is JAXA's.
- Rover numbers differ by source: about 40 m drop (DLR news; the DLR project page says 40 to 100 m), about three months (DLR) or 100 days (CNES). Name the source if shown.
- Spacecraft mass is 4,480 kg (JAXA); ignore ESA's 'around 4200 kg'.
- Landings: the safe wording is that the spacecraft lands on Phobos for a few hours (JAXA's science page says two soft landings).
- 'Idefix' is a registered name tied to Asterix: the rover may be named and drawn as a machine, never as an Asterix-style character or dog.
- No NASA, JAXA, ESA, CNES or DLR logos or insignia anywhere in the frames (NASA forbids its insignia next to AI-generated imagery, and none of these logos are ours to use).
