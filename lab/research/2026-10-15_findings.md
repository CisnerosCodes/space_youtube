# Research findings, 2026-10-15 (three subagent reports, condensed)

Limits: Reddit and most X replies were not reachable; many sources are guides or vendor blogs. Treat single-source items as unverified.

## 1. What makes viewers call a Short "AI"
1. Flat AI voice: no breaths, even pacing, odd emphasis, mispronounced names, the same stock voice across channels. ("The second I hear an AI voice, my thumb instinctively swipes away." https://patsgro.substack.com/p/i-hate-your-ai-voiceover)
2. Visuals that do not match the narration (generic stock or AI images). https://slopdetector.org/blog/youtube-ai-slop
3. Templated hooks and identical structure every day. https://www.vozo.ai/blogs/youtube/youtube-ai-slop
4. Vague or wrong facts; unsourced claims. Kurzgesagt found about 20% of AI research output unsourced. https://tildes.net/~tech/1qld/ai_slop_is_killing_our_channel_kurzgesagt
5. Generated footage artifacts (morphing, gibberish text). Space is a trust-sensitive niche (fake Avi Loeb channel removed). https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-fake-avi-loeb-youtube
6. YouTube now asks some viewers whether a video "feels like AI slop" (March 2026). https://dailydot.com/youtube-prompt-asking-does-this-feel-like-ai-slop

## 2. What people dislike in AI-coded motion
1. The same video every time: centered text, gradient, everything fading in. https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-opus-55-is-good-at-explainer
2. Everything on one curve, no hierarchy, never settles. https://aiartimind.com/how-to-make-motion-graphics-with-claude-opus-5-5/
3. Slideshow feel: "Unrelated reveals in sequence feel like a slideshow however well each one is animated." https://github.com/echris6/motion-video-kit ; "looks like animated slides, not movies" https://dev.to/dishant0406/i-asked-for-a-video-claude-motion-gave-me-an-html-file-459o
4. Filler labels and fake HUD/telemetry text (inference, matches the owner's own reaction to "DIAGRAM · NOT TO SCALE").

How to make shots flow (all doable in our renderer):
- One persistent actor that moves or morphs across beats (a marker ring, the echo mark, the photo itself).
- Carry motion direction across cuts; overlap shot tails and heads by 6 to 10 frames; match positions across a cut.
- Parallax depth on photos (cut into 2 to 3 layers, slow 3 to 5% push at different speeds) instead of a flat zoom.
- Foreground element becomes the transition (scales past camera revealing the next scene).
- Motion blur from sub-frame sampling or velocity-tied directional blur.
- Morph numbers and shapes instead of replacing them.
- Fast exits, slow arrivals; stagger related elements 3 to 6 frames.

## 3. Mascots and faces
- No controlled data that a mascot lifts CTR, retention or subscribers. A 300,000-video analysis found faces vs no faces roughly equal on average. https://www.searchenginejournal.com/do-faces-help-youtube-thumbnails-heres-what-the-data-says/563944/
- Shorts thumbnails are rarely seen in the feed; a mascot's thumbnail value is mostly for long-form. In Shorts it must work in the first second.
- AI cat content (2025 "cat soap operas") is a recognized slop genre. https://www.npr.org/2025/08/28/nx-s1-5493485/ai-slop-videos-youtube-tiktok
- A clearly cartoon character does not need YouTube's synthetic-content label. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491
- Risk: AI voice + AI character + daily template is close to YouTube's "inauthentic content" pattern (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392). Mitigate with sourcing on screen, varied formats, and a code-drawn (not generated) character.
- Cheapest credible test: one flat vector character with 5 to 8 expressions; long-form thumbnails A/B with and without it in YouTube Test and Compare (4 to 6 uploads); Shorts alternate cat reaction in the first 2 seconds vs none; judge on watch time and subscribers per 1,000 views, not CTR alone.
