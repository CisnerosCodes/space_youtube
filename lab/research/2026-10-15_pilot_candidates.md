## Part 1. JUST DISCOVERED

### JD-1. Webb measures the farthest fast radio burst and finds an unexpected host (RECOMMENDED)

1. **Headline and date.** "Webb Measures Distance to Farthest Fast Radio Burst, Suggesting Origin", NASA Science, published Oct 8, 2026 (14:00 EDT).
   - Primary (release): https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/webb-measures-distance-to-farthest-fast-radio-burst-suggesting-origin/
   - Primary (paper): Caleb et al., Science, published online Oct 8, 2026, DOI 10.1126/science.adz2675 (DOI link is on the ESA/Webb release: http://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adz2675; title "A fast radio burst at redshift 2, three billion years after the Big Bang" per Sci.News). A preprint exists: arXiv:2508.01648 (Aug 3, 2025), "A fast radio burst from the first 3 billion years of the Universe".
   - Secondary: ESA/Webb weic2621, 8 Oct 2026, https://esawebb.org/news/weic2621/ ; Live Science, Oct 8, https://www.livescience.com/space/astronomy/james-webb-telescope-pinpoints-the-most-distant-fast-radio-burst-ever-seen-more-than-doubling-the-previous-record ; Sci.News, Oct 12, https://www.sci.news/astronomy/most-distant-fast-radio-burst-15124.html (says it "more than doubl[es] the previous distance record").
2. **Most surprising verifiable fact.** "The team discovered that the host galaxy of FRB 20240304B was not typical of other galaxies with FRBs. Most FRB galaxies are massive star-forming galaxies, but the galaxy they found was 1,000 times less massive than they expected." Also: "Webb's NIRCam ... detected a galaxy in the right location, and its NIRSpec ... provided a precise measurement of the galaxy's redshift: 2.148, corresponding to a time just 3 billion years after the big bang." And the limit before Webb: "the world's largest ground-based telescopes could not see any galaxy at that spot in the sky."
3. **Hook for a non-space-fan.** A flash that lasted a thousandth of a second came from when the universe was about a fifth of its present age, and it traces back to a tiny, young galaxy nobody expected.
4. **Visuals and licenses.** All show what the story says, with one caveat: the burst itself is invisible (radio, millisecond long), so the only real picture is the faint host galaxy at the white cross. Use the inset.
   - NIRCam image with white cross and zoom inset (4353 x 3792 px): https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/webb/science/2026/10/STScI-01KKCJGBWX6TE4ABRTFWE9B8SQ.jpg . Credit: "Image: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI, Themiya Nanayakkara (USYD); Image Processing: Joseph DePasquale (STScI)". Asset page https://science.nasa.gov/asset/webb/frb-20240304b-nircam-image/ .
   - Same field with compass, scale bar, color key (4353 x 4734 px): https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/webb/science/2026/10/STScI-01M48S6Y7FR6103Z36417NZP3F.jpg . Credit: "Image: NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI, Manisha Caleb (SIfA); Image Processing: Joseph DePasquale (STScI)".
   - Spectrum graphic "Farthest Fast Radio Burst Host Galaxy Spectrum" (3840 x 2160 px): https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/webb/science/2026/10/STScI-01KKBYH5R2ACPDJF5SSX6N3FPJ.jpg . Credit: "Illustration: NASA, ESA, CSA, Joseph Olmsted (STScI); Science: Manisha Caleb (SIfA)". This is a graphic, not a photo; label it as such.
   - ESA/Webb copies (clean, annotated, spectrum), credit "NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI, T. Nanayakkara (USYD). Image Processing: J. DePasquale (STScI)": https://cdn.esawebb.org/archives/images/screen/weic2621a.jpg to weic2621d.jpg (the `d` file is the clean image per the page title "NIRCam clean image"). ESA/Webb terms (fetched https://esawebb.org/copyright/): "released under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license ... provided they are clearly and visibly credited", with "the wording unaltered".
   - License verdict: NASA/STScI originals are NASA content (generally not copyrighted in the US, acknowledge NASA as source, no implied endorsement). The ESA/Webb copies are CC BY 4.0, credit required. Safe path: use either one and always print the full credit line shown above. Scientific papers are not covered by the CC license.
5. **What happens next.** No dated event in the sources. The release says only: "They estimate that the MeerKAT telescope may be able to detect and localize several FRBs per year at a redshift greater than 1.0." For a NEXT card, a dated item does not exist; use a text card such as "More distant bursts: several a year, says the team" with no date, or skip the NEXT card for this episode.
6. **Risk notes.**
   - The burst was detected on March 4, 2024, and the preprint is from Aug 2025. What is new on Oct 8, 2026 is the peer-reviewed Science paper and the NASA/ESA release. Do not say "just detected".
   - The origin is "suggested", not solved. Quote: "Our work suggests that it's very unlikely that this FRB was produced by a merger." Do not say "magnetars cause FRBs".
   - Distance wording varies. NASA and ESA say "3 billion years after the big bang" (redshift 2.148). Live Science and Sci.News say "more than 10 billion light-years" or "traveled more than 10 billion years". Use the NASA wording only.
   - "Doubles the previous record" comes from the paper abstract ("doubles the redshift reach of localized FRBs") and secondary outlets, not from the NASA release text. Quote the abstract if used.
   - MeerKAT's "several per year" is redshift greater than 1.0, not "farther than this one". Do not imply more record breakers.

### JD-3. Chandra explains the "Treble Clef" in a galaxy cluster collision (Oct 14, the freshest item)

1. **Headline and date.** "NASA's Chandra Breaks Down 'Treble Clef's' Galaxy Cluster Collision", NASA Science, Oct 14, 2026 (12:46 EDT).
   - Primary (release): https://science.nasa.gov/missions/chandra/nasas-chandra-breaks-down-treble-clefs-galaxy-cluster-collision/ ; Chandra mirror: https://chandra.si.edu/blog/node/976
   - Primary (paper): Botteon et al., "The Treble Clef radio phoenix and its old nonthermal filaments", arXiv:2607.06659 (v1 Jul 7, 2026; v2 Oct 5, 2026; "Accepted for publication in A&A"). NASA says it was "published Wednesday in the Astronomy & Astrophysics journal".
   - Secondary: no independent outlet found yet. The arXiv abstract is an independent description by the authors.
2. **Most surprising fact.** "The resemblance to a treble clef is a case of pareidolia, the phenomenon when people see familiar shapes in random data, since there is no scientific connection to the musical notation." Also: "the formation lies almost directly behind the plane of the Milky Way, meaning that stars, gas, and dust from our home galaxy largely block the view from Earth."
3. **Hook.** A giant treble clef in space is real, and it is the glowing leftover of a black hole burp that two colliding galaxy clusters switched back on.
4. **Visuals and licenses.**
   - Composite (7200 x 5361 px): https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/chandra/2026/trebleclef.jpg . Credit: "X-ray: NASA/CXC/INAF-IRA/A. Botteon et al.; Infrared: UKIDSS-GPS ; Radio: ASTRON/LOFAR; Image processing: NASA/CXC/SAO/N. Wolk". I viewed it: pink radio filaments shaped like a clef over a blue-purple X-ray cloud. It shows exactly what the story says.
   - License verdict: NASA/CXC product, free to use with credit under NASA guidelines. It includes radio (ASTRON/LOFAR) and infrared (UKIDSS) layers from other institutions, so keep the full credit line. If in doubt, confirm with the Chandra X-ray Center (Megan Watzke, mwatzke@cfa.harvard.edu, listed on the release). Mark as "unclear for the third-party layers" until confirmed.
5. **What happens next.** No dated event. The paper calls the source "an ideal target for high-resolution, very-low-frequency follow-up observations with LOFAR2.0", with no date.
6. **Risk notes.**
   - Wording is stronger in the NASA release ("likely caused by an outburst from a supermassive black hole, followed by a collision") than in the paper abstract ("We speculate that its complex morphology is shaped by gas motions"). Keep "likely" or "researchers think".
   - The shape is a coincidence (pareidolia). Say so, or the video invites a false "message" reading.
   - Colors are false color, a composite of X-ray, radio and infrared.
   - Weaker "so what" for a general audience than JD-1 and JD-2.

## Part 2. BY THE NUMBERS

### BTN-1. Perseverance passes Opportunity as the longest-driving vehicle on another world (RECOMMENDED)

1. **Headline and date.** "NASA's Perseverance Sets Off-World Record for Total Distance Driven", published Oct 12, 2026 (record set Oct 11, sol 2,006).
   - Primary: https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/perseverance-mars-rover/2026/10/12/nasas-perseverance-sets-off-world-record-for-total-distance-driven/
   - Secondary: NASA Perseverance mission page repeats the same record text ("Record-Setting Rover: Perseverance Goes the Distance"), https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-2020-perseverance/ . Same agency, so this is not independent. JPL pages returned 403 to my fetch, and I found no independent outlet carrying the October numbers. Treat as single-source (NASA/JPL) but internally consistent between the article and the infographic.
2. **The numbers, quoted.**
   - "On Oct. 11, the rover's odometer reached 28.10 miles (45.22 kilometers), eclipsing the record of 28.06 miles (45.16 kilometers) set by NASA's Opportunity rover, which explored Mars from 2004 to 2018. Perseverance reached the mark on the 2,006th Martian day, or sol, of its mission, covering in five and a half years a distance that took Opportunity almost 15."
   - "Where NASA's first Mars rover, Sojourner, assessed the path ahead using 20 measurement points, Perseverance processes 600,000 with every look."
   - "The six-wheeled scientist has driven more than 90% of its total distance using AutoNav to evaluate the terrain, compared with about 5% for the first Martian AutoNav system on Opportunity and its twin, Spirit."
   - "The original mission called for no more than 12.4 miles of driving."
   - The record was passed during "a 299.6-foot (91.31-meter) drive".
3. **Hook.** A self-driving robot beat the old distance champion of another world in well under half the time. The reason is autonomy: it picks its own path while rolling.
4. **Visuals and licenses.**
   - Infographic "Out-of-this-world records!" (4500 x 8000 px, portrait, fits 9:16): https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/mars2020/2026/JPL_DrivingDistanceRecord_GraphicRecreation_August2026_v3.png . Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech. I viewed it. It plots Perseverance 45.2 km, Opportunity 45.16 km, Lunokhod 2 39 km and a marathon line at 42.195 km.
   - NAVCAM image from the record sol, Oct 11, 2026 (1288 x 968 px, PIA26812): https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/missions/mars2020/2026/perseverance-rover-updates/e1%20-%20PIA26812%20-%20NAVCAM%20record.png . Credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech. Real rover photo of the exact day.
   - Animated orbital-map route from 2021 to Oct 11, 2026 (credit NASA/JPL-Caltech) is embedded in the release; I did not locate a standalone file URL.
   - License verdict: NASA/JPL imagery, public domain under NASA media guidelines (do not use NASA logos in a way that implies endorsement).
5. **What happens next.** No firm dated event. From the release: "NASA recently approved the mission's first extension, which began in October and will carry Perseverance's exploration of the crater rim through roughly the end of 2028", and the rover is heading toward "Idubi", where the team hopes to find "traces of ancient hydrothermal systems". For the NEXT card use "Extended mission: through about the end of 2028" (approximate, say so).
6. **Risk notes.**
   - The margin is small: 0.06 km (about 60 m). The infographic rounds Perseverance to 45.2 km next to Opportunity 45.16 km, which looks almost tied. Use the article figures 45.22 vs 45.16.
   - The infographic is headed "as of October 12, 2026", while the article's figures are as of Oct 11. The infographic shows Curiosity at 38.15 km while the article says 38.07 km. Do not show the Curiosity number.
   - Opportunity's dates: the article says 2004 to 2018, and search results note that another JPL page ties the record to a 2019 end of mission. Use only "almost 15 years" from the quote, not a year range.
   - "Five and a half years" is NASA's rounding.
   - Rover marathon comparison: Opportunity already did a marathon in 2015 (NASA). Do not claim Perseverance is first to a marathon.
